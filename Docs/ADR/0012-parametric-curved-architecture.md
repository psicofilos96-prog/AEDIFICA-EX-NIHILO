# ADR 0012 — Parametric curved architecture (P0.11)

Arch, Vault and Dome are independent `PieceData` types. `PieceTransform.Position` remains the base reference and yaw remains the only stored orientation. All dimensions are meters, finite and at least 0.1 where applicable. A technical upper bound of 10,000 m per authoritative dimension keeps float mesh products finite; it is not an ergonomic rule. Semantic constructors reject profiles whose inner radii or opening would disappear. None of these pieces creates building connections or support structures.

## Contracts and profiles

- **Arch:** Width is the outer local X extent, Height the outer local Y extent, Depth the local Z extrusion. PierWidth, ArchRise and CrownThickness are authoritative. The derived opening is `Width - 2 PierWidth`; the spring line is `Height - CrownThickness - ArchRise`. The inner elliptical half arc is `x = (OpeningWidth/2) cos(t)`, `y = SpringHeight + ArchRise sin(t)` for `0 <= t <= pi`. Its apex is `Height - CrownThickness`, leaving a solid crown. Both spring height and opening width must be at least 0.1 m. The two pier bases sit at Y=0. The profile is a clockwise, concave XY polygon around the solid material and the bottom opening. It is triangulated once for each Z end; only boundary walls are extruded.
- **Vault:** Width is the *outer* span across X; Height is outer rise, Length runs along centered Z, Thickness is the reduction in both ellipse radii. Outer radii are `(Width/2, Height)` and inner radii `(Width/2 - Thickness, Height - Thickness)`. Both inner horizontal diameter and inner rise must remain at least 0.1 m. XY half ellipses are extruded along Z into a closed solid shell. Its longitudinal lower edges and end shell sections are capped, while the space beneath and the two end portals remain free.
- **Dome:** Diameter is the outer base circle, Rise is outer apex height, Thickness reduces both the horizontal radius and rise of an inner half ellipsoid. Inner diameter and inner rise must remain at least 0.1 m. Base is Y=0, horizontal center is X=Z=0. Outer and inner shells join at a bottom annulus; the center below the inner shell remains empty.

This is *elliptical radius reduction*, not a constant Euclidean normal offset. Thickness denotes the reduction of each principal radius and stays positive everywhere in the accepted profile. Vault and Dome may be flatter or taller than a semicircle/hemisphere. Arch CrownThickness is separate from PierWidth and ArchRise so the crown never reaches zero thickness.

`CurvedProfile` supplies samples to both mesh and snap. Fixed, internal resolution is 24 Arch arc segments, 24 Vault arc segments, and 32 longitude × 12 latitude Dome segments. There is no per-segment GameObject or user tessellation parameter. The mesh is rebuilt only when dimensions change. `PieceView` explicitly creates one nonconvex `MeshCollider`, shares the generated mesh with it and disables the bounding `BoxCollider`; there is no Rigidbody. Pose, material and selection changes retain the mesh, and localScale remains one.

## Editing and snapping

X/Y/Z Resize edits Arch Width/Height/Depth and Vault Width/Height/Length. Arch PierWidth, ArchRise and CrownThickness, Vault Thickness, and Dome Thickness remain fixed during this phase. Dome X edits Diameter, Y edits Rise. Z would edit the same Diameter, so its duplicate gizmo is hidden. Face Resize preserves the opposite face on the edited axis; when Diameter changes through X, its Z extent changes symmetrically around the base. The type-specific minimum of each editable dimension keeps the inner profile valid. Grid and Angle Snap use the existing transform/manipulation path.

Surface, Edge and Endpoint Snap expose bounded, deterministic samples from the same curves. Arch includes pier bases, outer corners, inner apex, outline edges, planar pier/end regions and top/outer sides. Vault includes lower ends, crown and selected outer facets and end shell sections. Dome includes the apex, cardinal rim points, true rim segments and sampled shell triangles. A straight chord spanning the opening is never exposed as a snap edge. Existing capture/release radius, self-exclusion and tie-breaking apply. These features are architectural samples, not a solver or exhaustive tessellation traversal.

The known z-fighting of overlapping independent pieces remains outside this phase. ConstructionLab contains one example of each new type. No new input binding is required.
